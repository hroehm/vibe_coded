Input / Output Interfaces

Master the physical and wireless connections that allow hardware devices to communicate. Review this briefing before moving to the Lab Activity.

Networking Interfaces

  • Ethernet (RJ-45): The standard wired connection for Local Area Networks (LANs). Uses twisted pair cables (Cat5e, Cat6). Highly reliable and fast (up to 10 Gbps).
  • Bluetooth: Short-range wireless tech used for Personal Area Networks (PANs). Connects peripherals like mice, keyboards, and headphones.
  • NFC (Near-Field Communication): Ultra short-range wireless (approx. 4 cm). Primarily used for contactless payments and quick pairing.
RJ-45 vs RJ-11: Do not confuse RJ-45 (Ethernet, 8 pins, wider) with RJ-11 (old landline telephones, 4 or 6 pins, narrower).
Tapping your smartphone to a credit card reader at a coffee shop uses NFC. Connecting your wireless headset to your phone uses Bluetooth.

Networking Tools

  • Crimper: A physical, pliers-like tool used to attach connectors (like an RJ-45 plug) to the ends of network cables. It presses the metal pins into the wires.
  • Cable Tester: An electronic device used to verify the integrity of network cables. It tests for continuity, breaks, and miswirings (crossed wires).
Without a crimper, you cannot make custom-length ethernet cables. A cable tester saves hours of troubleshooting by instantly telling you if a cable is physically broken.
A technician runs 100ft of Cat6 cable through a ceiling. Before plugging it into the expensive router, they use a Cable Tester on both ends to ensure none of the inner wires broke during the pull.

Peripheral Interfaces

  • USB-A: The standard, rectangular port found on older PCs and laptops for mice, keyboards, and flash drives. Not reversible.
  • USB-B: Squarish port with beveled top corners. Almost exclusively used on peripheral devices like desktop printers.
  • USB-C: The modern standard. Oval-shaped and completely reversible. Supports high-speed data, high-wattage power delivery, and video output.
  • Lightning: Apple's proprietary reversible connector used on older iPhones and iPads (prior to the shift to USB-C).
USB (Universal Serial Bus) revolutionized computing by providing "plug-and-play" capability, eliminating the need for various obscure serial and parallel ports.
You buy a new ultra-thin laptop. It likely only has USB-C ports because they are small enough for thin designs, yet powerful enough to charge the laptop and run external displays simultaneously.

Display Technologies

  • VGA: Very old standard. 15-pin, usually blue connector. Transmits analog video only. No audio.
  • DVI: Digital Visual Interface. Replaced VGA. Usually white connector. Digital (and sometimes analog) video only.
  • HDMI: High-Definition Multimedia Interface. The ubiquitous standard for TVs and monitors. Carries both digital video and audio over a single cable.
  • DisplayPort: Primary competitor to HDMI in the PC space. Highly versatile, supports daisy-chaining multiple monitors, high refresh rates, and audio.
Knowing what ports transmit audio is a common exam topic. VGA and DVI do NOT transmit audio natively. HDMI and DisplayPort DO transmit audio.
Setting up a home theater PC to connect to a television? You will absolutely use an HDMI cable to easily send both the 4K picture and the surround sound to the TV with one cord.
